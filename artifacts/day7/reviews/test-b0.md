**Trả lời:** Socrates counters Thrasymachus's claim that rulers pursue their own advantage by using the craft analogy. He argues that just as a craftsman (like a physician or a shipbuilder) works for the benefit of their craft and the good of their patients or ships, rather than for their own personal gain, so too should rulers govern for the benefit of their subjects rather than their own advantage. This implies that true justice is aligned with the common good, not merely the interests of the stronger or rulers. Socrates points out that if rulers command things that are not in their own interest, then justice cannot simply be defined as the interest of the stronger, as Thrasymachus suggests. Thus, he concludes that justice must be something more than mere obedience to the commands of the stronger, as it involves the well-being of the whole community, akin to how a craftsman prioritizes the integrity of their craft over personal gain [rep-b1-u018][rep-b2-u002].

**Trả lời:** War arises in the luxurious city because the original healthy State is no longer sufficient, leading to an expansion of needs and desires that exceed natural wants. This expansion necessitates the acquisition of more resources, which in turn leads to conflict with neighboring states over land and wealth. As Socrates explains, when people give themselves up to the unlimited accumulation of wealth, they will inevitably seek to take from others, resulting in war. Thus, war is derived from the causes of many evils in states, both private and public, as the city grows and requires more than it can sustain on its own [rep-b2-u018][rep-b2-u019].

**Trả lời:** The myth of the metals does not make class membership strictly hereditary because it introduces the idea that individuals can possess different types of metals (gold, silver, bronze) in their souls, which determines their role in society. This means that while there is a tendency for class membership to be hereditary, it is not absolute. This is illustrated in the discussions about the guardians and their selection based on their virtues and dedication to the State, rather than their birthright alone [rep-b3-u017][rep-b3-u038].
